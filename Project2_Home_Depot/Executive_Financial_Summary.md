# Home Depot financial performance analysis

## Executive recommendation

Maintain a neutral **hold / monitor execution** view. Home Depot reached record fiscal 2025 revenue of $164.7 billion, but the quality of that growth weakened: operating margin fell to 12.7% from 15.2% in fiscal 2021, net margin declined to 8.6%, and operating cash flow fell 17.6% year over year. The base case assumes low-single-digit revenue growth, gradual operating-margin recovery to 13.2% by fiscal 2028, and operating cash flow of approximately $19.0 billion.

The main decision trigger is margin stabilization. A sustained operating margin above 13% with improving cash conversion would support a more constructive view. Continued big-ticket demand weakness, acquisition-integration costs, or further inventory growth without matching sales would favor the downside case.

## What the numbers mean in human terms

Home Depot is not shrinking; it is becoming more expensive to operate. Revenue reached a new high, but the business kept less profit from each sales dollar because SG&A expanded while gross margin stayed broadly flat. That pattern points to execution pressure—labor, integration, and operating leverage—rather than a product-margin collapse.

The cash-flow picture is more reassuring than the income statement alone. The company still generated more operating cash than accounting profit in fiscal 2025, but inventory and receivables absorbed cash while payables provided less support. The practical question is therefore not “Can Home Depot grow?” but “Can it turn acquired growth into durable margin and cash conversion?”

The portfolio conclusion is intentionally conditional: a constructive upgrade requires Primary-segment growth, operating margin above 13%, CFO/net income above 1.0x, and improving ROIC. If those signals do not appear together, the downside case remains credible even if headline revenue continues to rise.

## Historical performance

- Revenue increased from $151.2 billion in fiscal 2021 to $164.7 billion in fiscal 2025, a 2.2% compound annual growth rate. Fiscal 2025 revenue grew 3.2%, helped by acquired businesses.
- Gross margin remained relatively stable near 33.3%, but SG&A rose from 16.8% to 18.6% of revenue. Operating margin declined 2.6 percentage points over the five-year period.
- Net income declined from $16.4 billion in fiscal 2021 to $14.2 billion in fiscal 2025 despite higher revenue. Interest expense increased to $2.4 billion, adding pressure below operating income.
- Operating cash flow exceeded net income in four of five years. Fiscal 2025 conversion was 1.15x, but operating cash flow fell to $16.3 billion from $19.8 billion.
- Free cash flow was approximately $12.6 billion in fiscal 2025 after $3.7 billion of capital expenditures, sufficient to cover $9.2 billion of dividends but with less headroom than in fiscal 2024.

## Liquidity, leverage, and working capital

- The current ratio improved to 1.06x, but the quick ratio remained low because inventory represents most current assets.
- Inventory increased $2.4 billion in fiscal 2025 while accounts payable decreased $0.4 billion and receivables increased $0.7 billion. This raised net operating working capital by about $3.5 billion and contributed to weaker cash flow.
- Debt represented approximately 48.8% of fiscal 2025 assets. Equity improved to $12.8 billion, reducing the distortion in debt-to-equity caused by the negative equity balance in fiscal 2021.
- The $17.6 billion SRS acquisition in fiscal 2024 and $5.4 billion GMS acquisition in fiscal 2025 increased assets, debt, integration requirements, and the importance of separating acquired revenue from organic demand.

## Advanced analytical findings

### Reported versus acquisition-adjusted growth

Fiscal 2025 total revenue increased $5.2 billion. Revenue attributed to the newer “Other” segment increased $6.3 billion, while Primary segment revenue declined $1.1 billion. The evidence therefore does not support interpreting reported growth as a broad recovery in the legacy business. The base case requires Primary segment revenue to return to positive growth before assuming that underlying demand has normalized.

### Operating-income attribution

The operating-income bridge separates four effects: revenue at the prior-year gross margin, gross-margin rate change, SG&A change, and D&A change. This decomposition shows that relatively stable gross margins did not offset the increase in operating expenses. SG&A growth is the main reason higher fiscal 2025 revenue did not produce higher operating income.

### Capital efficiency and earnings quality

ROIC is estimated using NOPAT divided by average invested capital, where invested capital equals interest-bearing debt plus equity less cash. The estimate is useful for trend analysis but is not identical to Home Depot's non-GAAP adjusted ROIC. Capital efficiency weakened as acquisitions increased invested capital while operating profit declined.

The accrual ratio, CFO-to-net-income conversion, FCF-to-net-income conversion, dividend coverage, and capex-to-D&A ratio provide complementary tests of earnings quality. Cash conversion remained above 1.0x in fiscal 2025, but the year-over-year decline and working-capital use justify closer monitoring.

## Scenario outlook

| Fiscal 2028 | Downside | Base | Upside |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue | $169.7B | $179.9B | $191.5B |
| Operating income | $21.0B | $23.8B | $27.2B |
| Net income | $14.2B | $16.2B | $18.9B |
| Operating cash flow | $15.6B | $19.0B | $23.0B |

The scenarios are directional, not valuation targets. They use revenue growth, gross margin, operating margin, cash conversion, capex intensity, a flat interest expense, and a stable effective tax rate.

The sensitivity analysis evaluates fiscal 2028 operating income across a 0%–6% three-year revenue CAGR and an 11%–16% operating-margin range. This avoids presenting the three named scenarios as precise predictions and makes the economic importance of margin recovery explicit.

## Assumptions and limitations

- Historical figures come from SEC Company Facts and Home Depot's fiscal 2025 Form 10-K. Values are in USD millions and follow Home Depot's 52/53-week fiscal calendar.
- SEC tags are standardized into analysis labels. Some cash-flow working-capital details were not consistently available as comparable Company Facts tags, so the analysis emphasizes balance-sheet changes and reported operating cash flow.
- Forecasts do not model transaction-level purchase accounting, acquisition synergies, store cohorts, comparable sales, ticket/transaction components, debt maturities, share count, or foreign exchange separately.
- The acquisition-adjusted analysis uses Home Depot's disclosed Primary and Other reportable-segment revenue. It is not a pro forma organic-growth calculation and does not remove all acquisition timing effects.
- Five annual observations are not sufficient for reliable causal inference or statistical regression. The driver analysis is an accounting decomposition, not a claim that expense changes caused demand changes.
- ROIC uses a consistent analytical definition for trend comparison. Differences from management's adjusted ROIC reflect lease, goodwill, average-balance, tax, and adjustment choices.
- Interest expense is held flat in the workbook forecast. Tax rate and D&A assumptions are simplified. Forecast balance sheets are not projected.
- Results are for analytical and portfolio purposes and are not investment advice.
